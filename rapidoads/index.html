05The EBITDA bridge

Two points of GMV. Nearly two points of EBITDA.

Non-ride revenue isn't equal-quality revenue. An ad rupee, a lending referral, an insights subscription — none of them carry captain payouts or incentives. At 75–90% contribution margin, 2% of GMV lands almost entirely on the bottom line.

Why 1.5% for ads? That's the Uber/Grab maturity band — benchmarked, defensible, boring in the best way. At ~$4B GMV that's ~$60M / ₹500 Cr a year of high-margin, recurring revenue. 02Four real pitches

Four brands. Four buyers. Four pricing models.

The proof it's a platform, not a media property: each pitch sells a different persona, on a different product, priced a different way. Tap a brand.

Budget 01 · brand

The brand business

Emotion, reach, occasions — sponsorships, takeovers, video in the wait and the cab. Bought by CMOs and agencies at premium CPMs. Sting and Big Billion Days live here.

Budget 02 · performance

The performance business

Outcomes, not impressions — installs, redemptions, verified walk-ins. Bought by growth teams and SMBs, self-serve at scale. The salon and the store launch live here.

A mature platform needs both: brand pays the premium CPMs; performance scales the advertiser count — and the Journey Graph powers each. One platform, two budgets, four proofs below.

Sting⚡
Sells: Ravi · Ankit · Manoj — the frequency audience

"Fuel the ride"

The insight: Sting's consumption moment is the commute — tired, hot, in motion, ₹20 in a UPI wallet. Rapido owns 4M+ of those moments every day, and 9M captains who live on energy drinks: an endemic audience nobody else can sell.

The campaign

Monday-morning sponsored commutes — Sting pays ₹10 off the fare and owns the most tired moment of the week. Wait-moment vernacular video across T2/3. Captain program on the 9M.

Measurement

Brand lift by exposure frequency; end-of-ride scratch card → ₹5 coupon redeemed at kiranas near the drop — offline redemption as the outcome signal.

Pricing

Sponsorship + CPM + per-redemption. Brand money that makes rides cheaper — the fare flywheel with a national FMCG behind it.

"You spend crores reaching youth between memes. We reach them mid-commute — the exact moment they drink you."
fFlipkart
Sells: Ankit first — the acquisition audience

The Big Billion Days takeover

The insight: Flipkart's war — Meesho on value, Amazon in the metros — is won on first-time and value shoppers in Tier 2/3. That's Rapido's home turf: the exact towns where Big Billion Days is decided, and where no measurable medium exists.

The campaign

Big Billion Days countdown takeover across 400 cities, vernacular-first. Deal-drops at ride-end ("your ride's done, the deal isn't"). App-install formats in the wait moment.

The audiences

Journey Graph segments: bike→auto graduates = rising spenders; T2/3 riders with no metro history = untapped first orders. Trajectory sold as acquisition targeting.

Pricing

Event sponsorship + CPI + audience-package PMPs — the programmatic lane agencies already know how to buy.

"Your next 100M customers don't watch IPL in English. They ride Rapido in Indore."
JH✂
Sells: Ankit · Sunita · Manoj — the self-serve long tail

Fill the 3pm chair

The insight: a salon chair is perishable inventory — an empty chair at 3pm is revenue lost forever. And a haircut repeats every 28–45 days: one converted customer is 12+ visits a year. ~900 franchises across T2/3/4, each fighting for local footfall.

The campaign

Dynamic off-peak coupons to riders passing within 500m at 2–5pm. Route-based "haircut on your way." The 28-day rebooking nudge timed to the last verified visit. Festive & wedding packs for Sunita.

Measurement

Pay-per-verified-walk-in at ₹20–40 — trivially ROI-positive against ₹150–400 tickets and 12-visit LTV. Observed arrivals, repeat rate: loyalty economics, not deal-hunting.

Pricing & channel

Per-visit + season packs, sold self-serve and assisted — the franchisee tops up ₹1,000 on a vernacular, WhatsApp-simple interface. No agency, no CPM jargon.

"An empty chair at 3pm is revenue you never get back. We fill it with the rider passing your door at 2:52 — and you pay only when he walks in."This pitch is the platform argument: thousands of small merchants = the self-serve engine that scales beyond a sales team.
zudio
Sells: Ankit · Sunita — the offline retail expansion budget

The only OOH network that knows if you walked in.

The insight: Zudio is opening a store a week in Tier 2/3 towns — and the only local media available is pamphlets, cable tickers, and an auto-wallah with a loudspeaker. Rapido is the first measurable offline medium at street level in these towns. The captain fleet is a moving OOH network. The app is precision geo-targeting. Together they are the one media product that can both create physical footfall and prove it happened.

The offline media play

Captain fleet as OOH: Rapido captains in Zudio's launch catchment carry branded kits — jacket, helmet, bike wrap — for the 2 weeks around store opening. Every ride in the 5km radius is a Zudio impression at street level. This is physical media in markets where no billboard company has inventory. Verified by Rapido's GPS — unlike a hoarding, you know exactly how many people saw it and where they were going.

Closing the loop

The captain OOH creates awareness. The app closes it: ride-end walk-in offer triggers when a rider drops within 500m of the new store. Sponsored auto to opening weekend — Zudio funds the fare, owns the journey. Footfall lift vs control towns is the proof metric — a per-store ROI number that Trent's CFO has never seen from a local media plan.

Why this beats the alternatives

Cable tickers reach 60+ audiences. Pamphlets are thrown away. Meta reaches metros. Rapido is the only medium that is physically present in the town, reaches the 18–35 buyer, and can measure the walk-in. For organised retail expanding at Zudio's pace, this is the difference between launching loud and launching blind.

"Every other medium tells you they reached Indore. We tell you which rider walked into your store — and which captain was wearing your logo when they passed it."

03Six partnerships. One shared user.

Not an ad network.
A mutual exchange.

Each partnership below works because both sides need something only the other can give. Rapido brings verified daily reach and the ride moment. The partner brings content, merchant access, or a product that makes riding with Rapido more valuable. The same Tier 2/3 user — more monetized, without a higher fare.

EdTech
The 15-minute commute is the only learning window Ankit and Manoj will realistically use. Seekho is built for exactly this — Hindi, practical, short. Neither company can replicate what the other already has.

—Why these six and not thirty
01
Same user, different moment
Every partner above serves the identical T2/3 rider — on the bike, at the temple, learning on the commute. No audience mismatch.
02
Rapido gets something back
Content that stickifies the wait (Pocket FM, AstroTalk), a sales force Rapido doesn't have (Lokal), or insurance that reduces captain churn (Acko). Not just money.
03
Partner can't replicate the moment
The ride-end cliffhanger, the temple-destination trigger, the captain identity — none of these can be bought on any other platform in India.
The rule: max 2 partner touchpoints per journey. The ride is the product — every partner interaction should make it feel more rewarding, never more cluttered.

04The build — from GAM to a platform

Today it's Google Ad Manager and a tiny sales team. That's the opportunity.

A billion impressions monetized at programmatic floor prices, sold through agencies, with no product or tech team behind it — the machine has simply never been built. Building it is the plan:

Phase 1 · months 0–6

Sell better before building much

Direct enterprise sales on existing inventory. Sponsorship products GAM can't serve — ride-moment natives, takeovers, sponsored commutes. Measurement wedge live. GAM stays as the backfill floor — revenue never dips while the machine gets built.

Phase 2 · months 6–18

The first-party platform

Own ad server for mode-aware formats. Self-serve, vernacular, WhatsApp-simple for the SMB long tail. Journey Graph audiences. Closed-loop reporting with observed visit evidence.

The year-one org — ~15–20 people
3–4 ad product 6–8 engineering 2–3 data science 5–6 sales · BLR / MUM / DEL 2 ad ops 2 B2B marketing
B2B marketing is category creation, not lead gen: CMO roadshows, agency-holdco partnerships (GroupM, Publicis desks), three lighthouse case studies with footfall numbers, and a presence at Goafest and ad:tech — because "journey media" is a category India's buyers haven't been sold before.
